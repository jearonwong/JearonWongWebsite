AI agents are not the insured subject.

The company can be insured. A person can hold authority. A contract can allocate responsibility. But the AI agent itself is usually not the legal subject that an insurance policy is built around.

That is why many AI insurance discussions feel incomplete. They focus on model outputs, logs, or framework traces, but risk transfer needs a stronger chain: who authorized the agent, what role it performed, what context it used, what controls shaped its behavior, what evidence survives reconstruction, and where coverage boundaries begin and end.

I have published a public research candidate for the third paper in the Agentic Lifecycle Governance Industry Series:

*Agentic AI Insurability & Risk Transfer White Paper 2026*

It connects WP1 on lifecycle compliance and WP2 on auditability and assurance to the next question: what would make agentic AI behavior legible for underwriting, claims, enterprise risk, and board-level governance?
