# Newsletter Draft: AI Agents Are Not the Insured Subject

**Status:** draft for owner review. Not executed.

AI agents are not the insured legal subject. They are agentic risk objects whose actions must be mapped back to human roles, corporate responsibility, coverage boundaries, and reconstructable lifecycle evidence.

That distinction matters because most AI insurance conversations still treat the agent as if it were the entity at risk. It is not. The company is exposed. Human roles remain accountable. Coverage language still has boundaries. Claims still require evidence that can survive reconstruction after an incident.

The new public research candidate, *Agentic AI Insurability & Risk Transfer White Paper 2026*, is the third paper in the Agentic Lifecycle Governance Industry Series. It extends the first white paper on lifecycle compliance and the second white paper on auditability and assurance into the risk-transfer question:

What evidence would an insurer, broker, reinsurer, counsel, CRO, CFO, CTO, board, or claims team need before treating agentic AI behavior as an intelligible enterprise risk?

The paper argues that logs alone are not claim evidence. Framework traces are useful, but they are not enough by themselves. Risk transfer needs a lifecycle evidence chain that connects system behavior to authorized human roles, governance decisions, runtime context, incident reconstruction, and coverage boundaries.

This is not a claim that agentic AI is automatically insurable. It is not an underwriting standard. It is not insurance advice, legal advice, a coverage opinion, or claims approval guidance. It is a public research candidate for how the evidence layer around agentic systems may need to mature before serious risk transfer can happen.
