# Repetition and Rhythm Audit Plan

## Purpose

R3H should make the assembled internal body read like one publication-grade whitepaper, not six stitched drafting waves. This plan identifies likely repetition and sets editing rules.

## Likely Repetition Zones

| Repetition zone | Why it repeats | R3H treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary disclaimers | Each drafting wave had to protect scope. | Preserve at first full definition, high-risk chapters, and Chapter 27; shorten elsewhere. |
| "Reviewability is not coverage" | Core non-claim discipline across underwriting, claims, and final model chapters. | Keep as a recurring thesis line, but avoid consecutive paragraph repetition. |
| WP1/WP2 bridge language | R3C translation layer and later continuity notes repeatedly restate the boundary. | Keep full bridge in Front Matter and Part III opening; later references should be one sentence. |
| Logs/traces vs evidence chain | Needed in Chapters 3, 6, 10, 19. | Keep first full distinction, then use short reminders. |
| Authority/evidence/remediation object lists | Object model, evidence architecture, renewal, remediation, and feedback chapters share components. | Keep full list in Chapter 24 object model; use targeted subsets elsewhere. |
| Public artifact non-claim language | Governance files require strong discipline. | Keep in reports and status files; do not import into body prose beyond necessary public-status caveat. |

## Repetition to Preserve

Some repetition is intentional and should not be erased:

- The insured legal subject / loss-relevant agentic work object distinction.
- Auditability is not insurability.
- Evidence supports reviewability but does not guarantee coverage, quote, premium, renewal, or claim payment.
- Policy wording, jurisdiction, facts, exclusions, limits, and claim handling remain external.
- AIO/AIRM-style models are authored analytical constructs only.

## Repetition to Compress

R3H should compress:

- Multiple part-opening summaries that retell every prior part.
- Repeated lists of forbidden claims inside normal body paragraphs.
- Repeated source caveats when a chapter already points to the final caveat register.
- Duplicative "MPLP is optional" language if MPLP remains absent or non-dominant.
- Multiple near-identical descriptions of work unit, authority, role, tool action, evidence, outcome, exception, remediation, and closure.

## Editing Rules

1. Preserve the first full definition of each core term.
2. Later references should be shorter and should point back conceptually rather than restating the full definition.
3. Never remove boundary language where claim risk increases.
4. Do not weaken Chapter 27 or the final non-claim boundary register.
5. Do not remove source caveats for exact policy wording, exclusion/sublimit signals, insurer claim documentation gaps, or underwriting questionnaire gaps.
6. Do not let compression create overclaim.
7. Keep chapter transitions clear enough that a reader feels forward motion.
8. Avoid internal wave language in the assembled body.
9. Preserve reader pain and scenario grounding; compress taxonomy before scenario.
10. Preserve thesis force in Front Matter, Chapter 1, Chapter 2, Chapter 24, Chapter 25, and Chapter 28.

## Rhythm Target

The assembled body should feel like an insurance/risk-engineering whitepaper:

- market reality first;
- object problem second;
- translation layer third;
- underwriting-facing architecture fourth;
- post-loss evidence fifth;
- final analytical models and conclusion last.

It should not read like a source matrix, internal protocol memo, auditability sequel, legal memorandum, underwriting checklist, or product brochure.
