## Jurisdiction Caveats

- Insurance filings vary by jurisdiction, line, policy form, date, regulator, and insurer adoption.
- NAIC model bulletin adoption is not uniform by default; use adoption map date and state caveats.
- EU AI Act is useful for regulatory context, but not a US insurance coverage source.
- SEC cyber rules apply to covered public-company disclosure obligations; they are not insurance claim rules.

## Marketing / Product Caveats

- Munich Re aiSure, Armilla, Chaucer, QBE, and Beazley product pages can support the existence of specific affirmative offerings, but cannot prove general AI insurability.
- Product availability, eligibility, covered object, limits, exclusions, and claim handling depend on policy terms not visible in public marketing.

## Law-Firm / Legal Commentary Caveats

- Law-firm sources may identify litigation and insurance issues but must not be treated as legal advice, coverage opinion, or binding interpretation.
- Use legal commentary only when primary law/regulator/policy sources are absent and only with an explicit caveat.

## Owner Review Needed

Owner review is recommended before any R2 or R3 claim that:

- names AIG, WR Berkley, or Great American as verified filing examples without primary filing text;
- turns AI cyber sublimits into a central market claim;
- uses product pages to suggest insurer acceptance of agentic lifecycle risk transfer;
- connects AIO/AIRM to underwriting without marking it as analytical synthesis.
