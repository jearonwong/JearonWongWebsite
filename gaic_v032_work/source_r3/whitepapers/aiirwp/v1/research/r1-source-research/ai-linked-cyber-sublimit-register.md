## Required Analytical Output

AI-linked cyber sources show that some AI-related losses can be partially transferred through existing or extended cyber products. They also show why the transfer is constrained. Coverage and claim review depend on the policy line, covered event, limit/sublimit, access path, incident response evidence, causality review, and whether the loss is cyber, technology liability, model performance, professional error, or something else.

LLMjacking is especially useful for WP3 because it is concrete: the loss may involve API credentials, unauthorized LLM use, cloud/compute charges, access logs, anomalous consumption, containment steps, and vendor dependency records. Those artifacts can support reconstruction. They still do not answer every insurance question: policy terms, exclusions, sublimits, causation, notice, mitigation, and loss measurement remain separate.

## R2 Use Rules

- Use QBE official materials as primary AI-linked cyber examples.
- Use Beazley Google Cloud as a cloud/AI service dependency example, not a universal AI coverage claim.
- Treat AI cyber sublimits as a source gap unless primary policy wording is found.
- Do not equate AI-linked cyber coverage with agentic lifecycle insurance.
- Keep "partially transferred but constrained by evidence, limits, sublimits, and causality review" as the safe analytical frame.
