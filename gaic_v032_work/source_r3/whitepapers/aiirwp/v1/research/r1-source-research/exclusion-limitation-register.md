## Analytical Output

Exclusions and limitations do not show that AI is uninsurable. They show that insurers and insurance infrastructure providers are trying to draw boundaries around what kind of AI-related loss can be transferred, on what line, under what conditions, and with what evidence.

For AIIRWP v1.0, the key analytical conclusion is not "AI is excluded." The safer conclusion is: AI risk transfer depends on defining the insurable object and evidence boundary. A GenAI liability endorsement, an exclusion, or an underwriting-flexibility filing is a signal that insurers need a way to distinguish model output, human decision, vendor tool, business process, covered subject, excluded activity, and post-loss causation path.

## R2 Use Rules

- Use Verisk as the primary exclusion/endorsement-development example.
- Use AIG / WR Berkley / Great American only as caveated reported examples until primary filings are obtained.
- Do not say all insurers are excluding AI.
- Do not say AI is uninsurable.
- Do not say exclusions are universal.
- Pair every exclusion signal with affirmative coverage and silent exposure signals so the market appears split, not one-directional.
