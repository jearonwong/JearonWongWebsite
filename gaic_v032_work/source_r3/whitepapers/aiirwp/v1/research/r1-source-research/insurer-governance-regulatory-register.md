## Required Analytical Output

Insurer governance expectations differ from enterprise risk transfer readiness. NAIC sources address how insurers should govern their own use of AI systems and how regulators may supervise that use. They are relevant because insurers themselves are being pushed toward documentation, controls, oversight, third-party management, and governance discipline when they deploy AI.

Enterprise risk transfer readiness is different. An enterprise seeking to transfer AI-linked risk must define its exposure inventory, authority scope, evidence chain, dependencies, loss scenarios, and reconstruction records. Regulatory AI governance can support that readiness, but it does not create coverage, set underwriting standards, or approve claims.

## R2 Use Rules

- Use NAIC as insurer-governance source, not coverage source.
- Use NIST AI RMF and EU AI Act as context only.
- Use SEC cyber rule to explain governance/disclosure exposure, not D&O coverage outcome.
- Do not treat AI regulation as insurance coverage proof.
