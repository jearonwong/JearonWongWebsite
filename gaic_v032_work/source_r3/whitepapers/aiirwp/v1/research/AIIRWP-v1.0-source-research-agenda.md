# AIIRWP v1.0 Source Research Agenda

R0 does not conduct full source research. R1 must verify each source before any claim enters the v1.0 body.

## Source Quality Requirements

- Prefer primary sources, regulator publications, insurer filings, official product pages, policy forms, annual reports, and reputable financial/insurance journalism.
- Record publication date, source authority, claim supported, overclaim risk, and exact citation target.
- Do not cite press or aggregator summaries for central claims when a primary source is available.
- Do not treat insurer/broker/product sources as neutral market-wide proof.
- Do not use technical framework docs as insurance-market evidence.

## 1. Insurance Market Exclusions / Retreat

Seed targets:

- AIG.
- Great American.
- WR Berkley.
- Verisk exclusion forms if available.
- Insurer filings / regulator filings where accessible.

Why it matters: v1.0 must show that the insurance market is already creating boundaries around AI risk rather than treating AI as a generic technology exposure.

Expected claim support: exclusion language, underwriting retreat, limitation of affirmative cover, and boundary conditions for AI-linked losses.

Overclaim risk: do not claim all insurers are retreating; distinguish filings, reported actions, specific product lines, and market-wide inference.

R1 tasks:

- Verify each named insurer signal with primary filing, regulator, official source, or high-authority financial/insurance journalism.
- Capture exact policy line, jurisdiction, form language, date, and whether the signal concerns AI generally, generative AI, cyber, E&O, D&O, or another line.

Citation quality: primary filing preferred; reputable financial press acceptable only when primary forms are inaccessible.

## 2. AI-Specific Coverage / Affirmative Coverage

Seed targets:

- Munich Re aiSure / insure AI.
- Armilla / AI warranty / model assurance.
- Lloyd's / AI cover examples if source-grounded.
- MKIII / mortgage AI screening insurance if source-grounded.
- CFC / Tokio Marine Kiln / other affirmative cover signals only if verified.

Why it matters: the paper must balance exclusion and limitation signals with affirmative cover and model-performance insurance examples.

Expected claim support: specific coverage examples, insured object, risk condition, performance warranty, covered loss type, and exclusion boundaries.

Overclaim risk: do not treat product availability as broad insurer acceptance of agentic AI risk.

R1 tasks:

- Verify each example against official product, insurer, partner, or broker documentation.
- Separate model-performance coverage from agentic lifecycle risk transfer.

Citation quality: official insurer / product pages preferred.

## 3. AI-Linked Cyber / Sublimits

Seed targets:

- QBE.
- Beazley.
- LLMjacking.
- Cyber policy sublimits.
- Regulatory breach limits if verified.

Why it matters: cyber is the most immediate line where AI-linked loss, model misuse, abuse of compute, and incident response evidence may appear.

Expected claim support: AI-linked cyber limitations, LLMjacking loss scenarios, sublimits, and how incident evidence affects coverage review.

Overclaim risk: do not equate cyber sublimits with complete AI insurability doctrine.

R1 tasks:

- Verify QBE and Beazley reports and whether they concern LLMjacking, AI-linked cyber, regulatory breach sublimits, or other mechanisms.
- Distinguish public reporting from official policy language.

Citation quality: official insurer/broker material or reputable insurance press; primary policy language when possible.

## 4. Silent AI Exposure

Lines to research:

- D&O.
- E&O.
- EPLI.
- Cyber.
- Media.
- Crime.
- Tech E&O.

Why it matters: AI risk may enter existing coverage silently before explicit AI products mature.

Expected claim support: silent exposure examples, management liability concerns, professional error concerns, data/privacy/cyber concerns, media/IP concerns, crime/fraud concerns.

Overclaim risk: silent exposure is a risk framing, not proof that coverage applies.

R1 tasks:

- Build a line-by-line exposure matrix.
- Identify what evidence each line would need after an AI-linked incident.

Citation quality: insurer, broker, reinsurer, regulator, or law-firm sources with clear boundary notation.

## 5. Claims / Incident Reconstruction

Seed targets:

- NIST SP 800-61r3.
- CISA incident response playbooks.
- SEC cyber disclosure.
- Claim evidence / causality / documentation sources.

Why it matters: claim review depends on reconstruction, documentation, causality framing, and incident handling evidence.

Expected claim support: incident response lifecycle, evidence preservation, disclosure timelines, event reconstruction, documentation discipline.

Overclaim risk: cybersecurity incident response is not the same as insurance claim approval.

R1 tasks:

- Map incident response evidence to claim reconstruction evidence.
- Separate causality trace from legal causation.

Citation quality: official NIST, CISA, SEC, insurer claim guidance, and legal/claims sources with caution.

## 6. Regulation / Governance

Seed targets:

- NAIC AI Model Bulletin.
- EU AI Act as context only.
- NIST AI RMF as context only.

Why it matters: insurance governance expectations shape how insurers use AI and how enterprises govern AI risk.

Expected claim support: insurer AI governance expectations, model risk governance, risk management lifecycle context.

Overclaim risk: regulatory governance sources are not coverage sources.

R1 tasks:

- Verify the NAIC Model Bulletin text and adoption status.
- Use EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF only as context, not as insurance coverage authority.

Citation quality: official regulator / standards sources.

## 7. Reinsurance / Accumulation

Seed targets:

- Geneva Association cyber accumulation.
- Systemic AI risk / model concentration.
- Reinsurance precedents.

Why it matters: reinsurability depends on aggregation, correlation, dependency concentration, and systemic loss modeling.

Expected claim support: accumulation risk concepts, systemic cyber analogies, model/vendor concentration, correlated failure.

Overclaim risk: do not claim actuarial pricing conclusions or reinsurer acceptance.

R1 tasks:

- Research cyber accumulation as analogy.
- Identify how model/tool/vendor concentration could create aggregation risk.

Citation quality: reinsurer, Geneva Association, regulator, academic, and insurance-market sources.

## 8. WP1/WP2 Internal Source Truth

Internal anchors:

- GAIC MRO / ALCS.
- AIAAWP Audit Evidence Chain / AARM.
- Authority Boundary.
- Accepted Outcome.
- Evidence Chain.
- Substitution Conformance.
- Remediation Closure.

Why it matters: v1.0 must close the series loop by translating WP1 and WP2 into insurability reasoning.

Expected claim support: lifecycle governance objects, audit evidence chain, auditability readiness, authority and accepted outcome mapping.

Overclaim risk: do not imply WP1/WP2 make systems insurable.

R1 tasks:

- Build WP1-to-insurability mapping.
- Build WP2-to-claim-evidence mapping.
- Mark all mappings as analytical synthesis until supported by external insurance sources.

Citation quality: internal canonical whitepaper sources plus explicit boundary language.
