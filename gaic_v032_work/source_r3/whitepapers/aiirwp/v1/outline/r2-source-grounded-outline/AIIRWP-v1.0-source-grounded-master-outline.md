# AIIRWP v1.0 Source-Grounded Master Outline

## Document Identity

**Title:** Agentic AI Insurability & Risk Transfer White Paper 2026
**Subtitle:** A Lifecycle Evidence Guide for Underwriting, Claims, and Enterprise Risk Transfer
**Status:** Source-grounded outline only. Not a body draft and not a publication artifact.

## Document Thesis

AI agents are not usually the insured legal subject. The insurable question is how the agentic risk object, a bounded lifecycle work unit, maps back to a legal subject, human responsibility role, coverage boundary, underwriting evidence package, claim reconstruction path, and lifecycle evidence chain.

## Audience

- Insurers and underwriting leaders.
- Reinsurers and accumulation-risk teams.
- Brokers and risk advisors.
- CROs, CFOs, CTOs, CIOs, counsel, boards, and AI governance leaders.
- Engineering implementation leaders, incident response teams, claims teams, and enterprise risk owners.

## Reader Promise

The paper will explain what can be discussed as insurable, what cannot be assumed insurable, what evidence an insurer or risk reviewer would need, how WP1 and WP2 translate into insurability reasoning, and why auditability is necessary but not sufficient for risk transfer.

## Relationship to WP1 and WP2

- WP1 / GAIC provides lifecycle governance objects: MROs, authority boundary, evidence partition, accepted outcome, substitution conformance, remediation closure, dependency visibility, and ALCS.
- WP2 / AIAAWP provides auditability objects: Audit Evidence Chain, Agentic Audit Object, evidence request logic, evidence sufficiency boundaries, and AARM.
- WP3 translates those objects into insurability reasoning only after establishing insurance-market reality. WP1/WP2 do not make systems insurable and do not bind insurers.

## Non-Claim Boundary

AIIRWP v1.0 must not claim publication, final/sealed status, insurer acceptance, coverage-ready status, underwriting-ready status, certification, legal proof, insurance advice, legal advice, coverage opinion, underwriting standard, actuarial pricing guidance, claims approval guidance, external adoption, indexing, SEO/GEO uplift, answer-engine recognition, or WP4 implementation.

## 20-Chapter Structure

### Front Matter

- Executive Thesis.
- Reader Map.
- Boundary and Non-Claim Note.
- Relationship to WP1 and WP2.
- How to read this paper.

### Part I: The Insurance Market Has Already Split AI Risk

1. **The New Insurance Question: What Exactly Is Being Transferred?**
   Establish the split between insured subject, covered object, loss event, evidence boundary, and policy line.
2. **What the Market Is Already Doing: Cover, Exclude, Sublimit, or Leave Silent**
   Use the R1 market signal matrix as the central table.
3. **Why LLM Insurance Is Not Agentic Lifecycle Insurance**
   Distinguish model-performance and AI cyber products from agentic lifecycle risk transfer.

### Part II: The Core Object Problem

4. **The Insured Subject: Company, Person, Officer, Professional, Vendor**
   Answer whether the insured object is company, human, agent, vendor, or work unit.
5. **The Agentic Risk Object: Bounded Lifecycle Work Unit**
   Define the analytical work unit without claiming external standard status.
6. **Human Role x Agent Role x Corporate Responsibility**
   Build responsibility bridge between human, agent/tool, corporate owner, and remediation owner.
7. **HITL Is Not a Responsibility Structure**
   Show why review events are insufficient without authority, acceptance, evidence, and closure records.

### Part III: From Compliance and Auditability to Insurability

8. **From WP1 MROs to Insurability Objects**
   Translate MROs into analytical insurability inputs.
9. **From WP2 Audit Evidence Chain to Claim Evidence Chain**
   Translate audit evidence into claim reconstruction input while preserving approval boundary.
10. **Authority, Accepted Outcome, Substitution, and Remediation as Insurance Objects**
   Explain the highest-value lifecycle evidence states for underwriting and claims reconstruction.

### Part IV: Underwriting Agentic AI

11. **The Underwriting Evidence Pack**
   Define evidence categories an insurer or risk reviewer would need for discussion.
12. **Premium Variables Without Pricing Claims**
   Provide analytical exposure variables without formulas, rates, premiums, or actuarial guidance.
13. **Dependency Concentration and Aggregation Risk**
   Use cyber/cloud accumulation analogy to show shared model/tool/vendor risk.
14. **Enterprise Exposure Inventory for Agentic Work**
   Convert object thinking into an inventory of work, authority, dependencies, incidents, and evidence readiness.

### Part V: Claims and Disputes

15. **Claim Reconstruction Evidence Package**
   Define incident timeline, technical trace, authority trace, causality trace, and remediation evidence.
16. **Responsibility and Coverage Boundary Review**
   Separate responsibility allocation from policy line, exclusion, sublimit, and coverage review.
17. **Remediation Closure and Post-Loss Evidence**
   Show how closure evidence supports dispute readiness without determining claim outcome.

### Part VI: AIIRWP Models

18. **Agentic Insurability Objects v2**
   Present AIO v2 as analytical object model, not standard or certification.
19. **Agentic Insurability Readiness Model v2**
   Present AIRM v2 as readiness vocabulary, not score, certification, or underwriting standard.
20. **Dispute-Ready Risk Transfer Architecture**
   Close with architecture for evidence-ready risk transfer discussions and later WP4 implementation planning.
