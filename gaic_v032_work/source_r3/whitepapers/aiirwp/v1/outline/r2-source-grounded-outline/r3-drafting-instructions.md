# R3 Drafting Instructions

**Status:** Instructions for a later formal body draft. R3 may begin only after R2 acceptance.

## Gate

R3 may draft the formal AIIRWP v1.0 body only after this R2 source-grounded outline is accepted. R3 must not treat R2 as a body draft and must not restore withdrawn v0.2 text as the body base.

## Drafting Posture

- Write from reader pain and market reality before internal vocabulary.
- Use concrete examples before abstraction.
- Build each chapter as an argument, not as a source list.
- Keep the split insurance market frame visible across the paper.
- Introduce AIO v2 and AIRM v2 only after Parts I-V have earned the need for them.
- Preserve all source-strength caveats and boundary notes from R1 and R2.

## Chapter Rhythm

Each chapter should include:

1. A reader-facing opening problem.
2. A concrete insurance or enterprise scenario.
3. The chapter thesis.
4. Three to five argument beats.
5. Source-grounded support with source notes kept clean.
6. WP1/WP2 bridge only where it helps the reader.
7. A closing bridge to the next chapter.

## Source Use

- Prefer primary or high-authority R1 sources for central claims.
- Use caveated sources only for context unless primary replacement is found.
- Do not cite aggregator summaries for central claims if primary source exists.
- Do not turn product marketing into market-wide proof.
- Do not treat technical framework documentation as insurance-market evidence.
- Do not treat law-firm commentary as legal conclusion.
- Keep source notes in the body clean; do not make the chapter read like a research register.

## Table and Figure Use

- Use tables for comparison, not as a substitute for argument.
- Keep high-comparison matrices as true tables in future HTML/PDF work where readability allows.
- Avoid row-card-only conversion for tables where comparison across columns is central.
- Use figures to clarify responsibility, dependency, evidence chain, and architecture relationships.
- Do not overload early chapters with internal model tables.

## Boundaries to Preserve

R3 must not claim:

- Publication, final/sealed status, current active public WP3 candidate, or public release.
- Insurer acceptance, coverage-ready status, underwriting-ready status, or certification.
- Insurance advice, legal advice, coverage opinion, legal proof, legal liability determination, or claim approval.
- Underwriting standard, actuarial pricing guidance, premium recommendation, pricing model, or rate effect.
- External adoption, endorsement, indexing/SEO-GEO uplift, answer-engine recognition, or WP4 implementation.
- MPLP requirement, Validation Lab certification, vendor ranking, or procurement recommendation.

## v0.2 Use Rule

The withdrawn v0.2 candidate may be referenced only as a failure case or do-not-use archive. R3 must not copy or adapt v0.2 as body truth. Isolated phrases may be reused only if independently justified by the R1/R2 source base and rewritten into the v1.0 structure.

## Depth Target

The R3 body should be publication-grade and materially deeper than the withdrawn v0.2 candidate. The target depth is comparable to AIAAWP or stronger, not a 20-30 page essay. The paper should feel like a mature insurance/risk-transfer guide, not a source pack, concept dump, or protocol marketing document.

## R3 Acceptance Preconditions

Before R3 is accepted, it should show:

- Reader pain at the beginning of each chapter.
- Market split clearly established before AIO/AIRM.
- WP1/WP2 translation expressed as analytical synthesis.
- Claims and coverage boundaries preserved.
- Premium variable discussion useful but non-pricing.
- Claims/disputes chapters free of claim approval or legal conclusion language.
- Appendices aligned to the body rather than carrying the argument alone.
