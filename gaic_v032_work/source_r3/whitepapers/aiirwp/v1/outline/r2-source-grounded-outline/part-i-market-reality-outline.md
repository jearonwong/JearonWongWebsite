# Part I Market Reality Outline

## Part Thesis

The insurance market has not made a single decision about AI. It is splitting AI risk across affirmative cover, exclusions/endorsements, sublimits/caps, silent exposure, cyber-linked cover, and model-performance warranty structures. That split forces WP3 to ask what subject, object, event, evidence boundary, and policy line are being transferred.

## Chapter 1: The New Insurance Question: What Exactly Is Being Transferred?

| Field | Outline |
|---|---|
| Chapter thesis | The useful first question is not "is AI insurable?" but "what risk object is being transferred, by whom, under which policy line, with what evidence?" |
| Reader pain | Risk leaders are asked whether AI is insured, but the answer changes depending on whether the loss is model performance, cyber abuse, professional error, D&O governance, product liability, or a vendor dependency. |
| Opening scene | A company deploys an AI support agent that refunds a customer, sends a wrong notice, triggers an API charge, and creates a complaint. The policyholder asks if "the AI incident" is insured, but the incident contains several possible objects and lines. |
| Argument beats | Introduce insured subject vs risk object; explain why a legal subject can be insured while the agentic work object remains ambiguous; name the five dimensions: subject, object, event, evidence, line; preview split market. |
| Primary sources | INS-VERISK-GL-2025; INS-QBE-AI-CYBER-2025; INS-MUNICH-AISURE. |
| Secondary/context sources | INS-AON-AI-2026; REIN-GENEVA-GENAI-2025. |
| Market signals used | Exclusion/endorsement development, affirmative AI cover, AI cyber cover, model-performance products. |
| Table/figure plan | Small "Insurance Transfer Question Stack" table: insured subject, risk object, event trigger, evidence, policy line, limit/exclusion. |
| WP1/WP2 bridge | WP1 offers lifecycle work objects; WP2 offers evidence-chain thinking. They appear later as answer paths, not starting assumptions. |
| Forbidden claims | Do not claim AI is insurable/uninsurable; do not give coverage opinion; do not claim insurer acceptance. |
| R3 drafting notes | Write from enterprise confusion. Do not introduce AIO/AIRM yet except as "later vocabulary." |

## Chapter 2: What the Market Is Already Doing: Cover, Exclude, Sublimit, or Leave Silent

| Field | Outline |
|---|---|

| Reader pain | Buyers hear contradictory messages: AI insurance exists, AI is excluded, cyber covers some AI loss, D&O may respond, warranties exist, and sublimits may apply. |
| Opening scene | A broker receives three client questions in one week: can we buy AI warranty cover, will our cyber policy respond to LLMjacking, and is GenAI excluded from GL? The correct answer is line-specific and evidence-specific. |

## Chapter 3: Why LLM Insurance Is Not Agentic Lifecycle Insurance

| Field | Outline |
|---|---|
| Chapter thesis | Model-performance warranties and AI-linked cyber coverage are important but narrower than agentic lifecycle risk transfer. |
| Reader pain | Executives may see "AI insurance" and assume it covers the whole lifecycle of agentic work, including authority, tool use, human review, vendor dependencies, and downstream action. |
| Opening scene | An AI vendor obtains performance warranty coverage for a model KPI, while the enterprise using the model suffers a workflow loss caused by delegated tool action and weak acceptance controls. Those are different insurance questions. |
| Argument beats | Separate model output/performance, AI product underperformance, cyber/LLMjacking, professional liability, and lifecycle work; explain covered object/event/trigger; introduce agentic lifecycle gap. |
| Primary sources | INS-MUNICH-AISURE; INS-MUNICH-AISURE-PDF; INS-MUNICH-AISELF; INS-ARMILLA-AI; INS-ARMILLA-2023; INS-CHAUCER-ARMILLA-2025; INS-QBE-AI-CYBER-2025. |
| Secondary/context sources | INS-UK-ARMILLA; REIN-GENEVA-GENAI-2025; INS-BEAZLEY-GCLOUD. |
| Market signals used | Model-performance warranty, affirmative AI cover, cyber-linked AI risk, agentic lifecycle gap. |
| Table/figure plan | "Model Performance vs Agentic Lifecycle Risk Transfer" comparison table. |
| WP1/WP2 bridge | WP1 authority/accepted outcome and WP2 Audit Evidence Chain show what model-performance cover does not necessarily capture. |
| Forbidden claims | Do not claim Munich Re, Armilla, Chaucer, QBE, or Beazley accepts AIO/AIRM/MPLP; do not generalize product examples into market-wide proof. |
| R3 drafting notes | Use concrete product examples, but keep marketing caveats visible. End by moving to the subject/object problem in Part II. |
