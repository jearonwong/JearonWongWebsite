# R2 Open Caveats

**Status:** Caveat register for R3 planning. These items do not block R2 acceptance, but they constrain R3 drafting.

## Source Caveats Carried from R1

| Caveat area | Current state | R3 requirement |
|---|---|---|
| AIG / WR Berkley / Great American AI exclusion details | Reported or secondary-source signals remain caveated where primary filings/forms are not available | Do not centralize exact wording unless primary source is obtained |
| Verisk exclusion form language | Verisk/ISO signal is useful, but exact form text may require owner/source review | Use form-development signal carefully; avoid quoting unavailable form wording |
| AI cyber sublimit wording | QBE and related AI cyber examples support partial-transfer framing, but exact sublimit terms remain limited | Discuss limits/sublimits as constrained market signal, not universal policy term |
| Insurer claim documentation | R1 found incident response and cyber reconstruction sources stronger than insurer-specific claim packet sources | Use NIST/CISA/QBE evidence context; caveat lack of insurer claim forms |
| Underwriting questionnaires | Public AI-specific underwriting questionnaires remain limited | Present evidence pack as analytical request structure, not insurer form |
| Policy form gaps | Many product/coverage examples do not expose full terms | Do not infer exclusions, conditions, limits, or claim treatment |
| Law-firm commentary | Useful only as context | Do not use as legal conclusion |
| Product marketing | Useful as product signal | Do not treat as market-wide proof or insurer acceptance of AIO/AIRM |

## Claims Requiring Owner Review Before Strong Wording

- Any named-insurer exclusion or limitation claim beyond source-visible language.
- Any exact policy form, exclusion, or endorsement wording.
- Any exact sublimit or cap statement.
- Any claim that a product covers a specific AI loss type beyond its public description.
- Any claim that a source indicates insurer appetite for agentic lifecycle risk transfer.
- Any proposed AIRM level names if they resemble scores, ratings, or certification.

## Claims to Avoid Until Better Source Exists

- "AI is uninsurable."
- "The market accepts agentic AI risk transfer."
- "Insurers require lifecycle evidence chains."
- "AIO/AIRM are underwriting standards."
- "Auditability makes a system insurable."
- "Complete logs are sufficient claim evidence."
- "This evidence package will support claim approval."
- "Premium variables can predict price."
- "Shared model/vendor dependency has a quantified premium effect."

## Primary Filing and Policy Form Gaps

R3 can proceed with caveats, but publication-stage review should recheck:

- Public regulatory filings or ISO/Verisk form references for AI exclusions.
- Public product terms for affirmative AI coverage examples.
- Public cyber policy language for AI-linked sublimits or compute-abuse treatment.
- Insurer or broker AI underwriting questionnaire examples.
- Insurer claim documentation examples specific to AI-linked incidents.

## Visual and Layout Risks for Future Artifact Stage

- Split Market Signal Matrix must remain a comparison table if possible.
- Insurance Line vs Agentic Exposure Matrix may be too wide for PDF unless redesigned.
- Premium Variable Dictionary must stay readable without implying formulaic scoring.
- AIO v2 and AIRM v2 tables should be visually distinct from standards/certification scorecards.
- Boundary Allowed/Forbidden Language Table should remain easy to scan and should not be buried.

## R2 Final Caveat

R2 is complete if R3 can draft without inventing unsupported claims. R2 does not eliminate the need for R3 source discipline, body QA, artifact planning, or owner review before any public release decision.
