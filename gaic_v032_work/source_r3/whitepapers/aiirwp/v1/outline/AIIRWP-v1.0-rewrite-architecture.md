# AIIRWP v1.0 Rewrite Architecture

## Required Thesis

AI agents are not the insured legal subject. They are agentic risk objects whose actions must be mapped back to legal subjects, human roles, corporate responsibility, coverage boundaries, underwriting variables, claim reconstruction evidence, and lifecycle governance records.

## Rewrite Direction

AIIRWP v1.0 must start from insurance market reality, not from MPLP or internal protocol vocabulary. WP1 and WP2 become the answer path only after the paper has established why the insurance market is splitting AI risk through affirmative cover, exclusions, sublimits, silent exposure, model-performance products, and cyber/technology liability pressure.

## Content Pillars

### 1. Market Reality

- Affirmative AI cover.
- Silent AI exposure.
- Exclusions.
- Sublimits.
- Insurer retreat / limitation.
- AI-linked cyber.
- Model performance insurance.

The paper must distinguish verified market signals from analytical interpretation. No unverified market claim enters the body.

### 2. Insured Subject

- Company.
- Professional.
- Director/officer.
- Vendor/service provider.
- Platform/operator.
- Why the agent is usually not the insured subject.

The insured subject question must anchor the paper. AI agents may be loss-relevant, but legal subject, policyholder, insured party, responsibility holder, and contractual counterparty remain distinct.

### 3. Agentic Risk Object

- Bounded lifecycle work unit.
- Authority scope.
- Tool/data/model dependency.
- Accepted outcome.
- Loss event.

The agentic risk object is the object being analyzed for evidence and loss reconstruction, not a claim that the object itself is insured.

### 4. Responsibility Bridge

- Human role.
- Agent role.
- Corporate responsibility.
- MAS role mapping.
- HITL vs responsibility structure.

The paper must show why "human in the loop" is not enough unless authority, review, acceptance, remediation, and closure responsibility are structured.

### 5. WP1 Mapping

- MRO to insurability.
- Authority boundary.
- Evidence partition.
- Accepted outcome.
- Substitution conformance.
- Remediation closure.
- Cross-project / dependency / aggregation risk.

WP1 mapping must be explanatory and reader-facing, not a dumped crosswalk.

### 6. WP2 Mapping

- Audit Evidence Chain to Claim Evidence Chain.
- AARM to underwriting evidence readiness.
- Audit evidence vs claim evidence.
- Assurance readiness vs risk transfer readiness.

WP2 mapping must clarify that auditability helps but does not equal insurability.

### 7. Underwriting

- Evidence an underwriter would need.
- Exposure inventory.
- Authority map.
- Control maturity.
- Loss scenarios.
- Dependency concentration.

The underwriting section must not become actuarial pricing guidance.

### 8. Premium Variables

- Frequency.
- Severity.
- Authority scope.
- Transaction value.
- Human review depth.
- Model/tool/vendor concentration.
- Evidence completeness.
- Incident history.
- Remediation latency.
- Aggregation exposure.

Premium variables must be discussed as analytical exposure variables, not pricing formulas or actuarial advice.

### 9. Claims

- Claim evidence package.
- Reconstruction chain.
- Causality trace vs legal causation.
- Coverage boundary review.
- Responsibility allocation.
- Remediation evidence.

Claims analysis must not become claims approval guidance, coverage opinion, settlement advice, or legal liability determination.

### 10. AIO/AIRM v2

- Object model.
- Readiness vocabulary.
- Not certification.
- Not insurer acceptance.

AIO/AIRM v2 should be usable as vocabulary for evidence readiness, not as a certification, score, or guarantee.

### 11. Dispute-Ready Risk Transfer Architecture

- What good looks like.
- Why it matters.
- How it connects to enterprise implementation WP4 later.

WP4 remains planned and not implemented.

AIIRWP v1.0 must not claim legal advice, insurance advice, coverage opinion, underwriting standard, actuarial pricing guidance, claims approval guidance, insurer acceptance, coverage-ready status, underwriting-ready status, certification, endorsement, Final Seal, external adoption, indexing, SEO/GEO uplift, answer-engine recognition, or WP4 implementation.
