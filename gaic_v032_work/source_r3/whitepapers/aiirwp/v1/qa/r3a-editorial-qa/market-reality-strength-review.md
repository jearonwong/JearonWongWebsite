# Market Reality Strength Review

**Decision:** strong with notes

## Assessment

R3A successfully grounds the paper in insurance market reality before internal vocabulary. The draft does not begin with AIO, AIRM, MPLP, or WP1/WP2 mappings. It begins with the insurance question: what exactly is being transferred?

The split-market frame is explicit and sustained:

- affirmative AI cover;
- exclusion / endorsement development;
- sublimit / cap signals;
- silent AI exposure;
- AI-linked cyber risk;
- professional liability ambiguity;
- D&O / governance exposure;
- claim reconstruction need;
- aggregation / reinsurance concern;
- model-performance warranty;
- agentic lifecycle gap.

The market examples are concrete enough for draft-stage acceptance. Munich Re, Armilla, Chaucer, QBE, Verisk/ISO, Aon, Geneva Association, Swiss Re, Beazley, NIST, CISA, SEC, and NAIC are used in the correct roles. Product examples are not stretched into market-wide proof.

The reader can see why WP3 is necessary: the market is not saying "AI yes" or "AI no." It is sorting AI into narrower products, exclusions, endorsements, limits, silent exposures, cyber events, model-performance objects, governance contexts, and reconstruction needs. That split creates the need for subject/object/evidence separation.

## Notes

- Chapter 2 is strong enough to anchor Part I, but dense enough that a later continuity edit should tune its pacing.
- The market matrix should remain a main-body table for now; Appendix A can later carry a fuller source register.
- Exact exclusion and sublimit wording remains a source gap and must not become central proof.

## Result

Strong enough for R3B with notes.
