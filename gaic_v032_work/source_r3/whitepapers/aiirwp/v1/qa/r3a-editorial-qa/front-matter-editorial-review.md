# Front Matter Editorial Review

**Decision:** keep with notes

## Review Scope

- Executive Thesis
- Reader Map
- Boundary and Non-Claim Note
- Relationship to WP1 and WP2
- How to Read This Paper

## Findings

The Front Matter works. It opens with a real thesis rather than a literature summary: the first insurance question is not whether "AI" is insured, but what exactly is being transferred. That sentence carries the v1.0 rewrite direction and immediately separates broad AI talk from insurance object discipline.

The insured subject vs risk object distinction is clear and early. The draft states that AI agents are usually not the insured legal subject and frames them as loss-relevant parts of a bounded lifecycle work unit. This is the correct center of gravity for the paper.

The reader promise is also clear. The paper promises to explain what can be discussed as an insurable object, what cannot be assumed transferable, what evidence an insurer or risk reviewer would need, how WP1/WP2 translate into reasoning, and why auditability is necessary but not sufficient.

The Reader Map is useful. It names insurers, reinsurers, brokers, CROs, CFOs, CTOs/CIOs, counsel, boards, AI governance leaders, and engineering implementation leaders, and each persona receives a concrete reason to keep reading. It is long, but not bloated; it earns its space because the whitepaper must serve multiple sides of the insurance conversation.

The Boundary and Non-Claim Note is safe and necessary. It may feel dense on first pass, but that density is understandable given the insurance/legal/claims risk surface. It should remain visible. Later line editing can reduce repetition, but not remove the boundaries.

The WP1/WP2 relationship is correctly positioned. WP1 and WP2 are introduced as evidence architecture and auditability bridge, not as insurance proof. The "auditability is necessary but not sufficient" line is strong and should remain.

## Notes

- Preserve the thesis force. The current opening is strong enough to anchor the whole paper.
- Later continuity editing may tighten the Boundary and Non-Claim Note so it reads less like a compliance block, while keeping every substantive caveat.
- The Reader Map can stay in R3A for now. If future full-body assembly feels long, consider tightening persona paragraphs rather than deleting the section.
