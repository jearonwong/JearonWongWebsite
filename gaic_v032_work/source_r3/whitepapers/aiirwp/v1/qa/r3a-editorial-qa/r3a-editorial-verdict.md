## Decision Rationale

R3A is publication-grade enough for R3B drafting to begin with notes. It is not a failed opening and does not need an R3A repair wave before Part II.

The draft clears the core editorial gates:

- Front Matter has a strong enough thesis: the first insurance question is not whether "AI" is insured, but what exactly is being transferred.
- Chapter 1 clearly establishes the distinction between insured legal subject and agentic risk object through a concrete refund/account/API workflow.
- Chapter 2 establishes split-market reality through affirmative cover, exclusion/endorsement development, sublimit/cap context, silent exposure, AI-linked cyber risk, professional liability ambiguity, governance exposure, claim reconstruction, aggregation, model-performance warranty, and the agentic lifecycle gap.
- Chapter 3 successfully prevents false confidence by separating model-performance insurance, AI warranty/product examples, AI-linked cyber, professional liability, technical traces, and agentic lifecycle risk transfer.
- Source use is clean enough for draft-stage acceptance: primary sources carry central claims, caveated sources remain caveated, and internal WP1/WP2 anchors are not treated as external insurance facts.
- Reader pain is present and concrete. The draft is built around the broker/risk/counsel/CTO question of what happened, who is insured, what object caused loss, and what evidence survives.
- WP1/WP2 bridges are useful and subordinate to market reality.
- Boundary risk is controlled.

## Minor Notes

These notes do not block R3B:
