# Chapter 2 Editorial Review

**Chapter:** What the Market Is Already Doing: Cover, Exclude, Sublimit, or Leave Silent
**Decision:** keep with notes

## Findings

Chapter 2 does the right job: it proves that WP3 is not inventing a theoretical problem. The chapter frames the market as split rather than binary, and it repeatedly avoids the dangerous conclusions that AI is broadly covered, AI is uninsurable, all insurers are excluding AI, or affirmative coverage proves agentic lifecycle risk transfer.

The three-client broker scene is effective. It separates an AI vendor seeking model-performance warranty support, a company facing LLMjacking/cloud cost exposure, and an enterprise wondering whether existing lines silently absorb GenAI exposure. That scene makes the market split feel practical.

The Split Market Signal Matrix is useful and source-grounded. It covers affirmative AI cover, exclusion/endorsement development, sublimit/cap, silent AI exposure, cyber-linked AI risk, professional liability ambiguity, D&O/governance exposure, claim reconstruction, aggregation/reinsurance concern, model-performance warranty, and agentic lifecycle gap. It distinguishes what each signal proves and what it does not prove.

The source caveats are handled correctly. Verisk/ISO is used as a primary form-development signal, not as universal exclusion proof. QBE is used for AI-linked cyber and LLMjacking context, not broad lifecycle coverage. Munich Re, Armilla, and Chaucer are used as product-specific examples. Aon is used for line-of-business context. FT-derived and law-firm/journalism-derived sublimit or exclusion signals are caveated as reported context, not primary proof.

The chapter has one editorial risk: density. The matrix plus the subsequent eleven-pattern prose is accurate, but it can feel heavy. Still, it does not become a source inventory because each pattern returns to the central object/evidence/boundary argument.

## Notes

- Keep the matrix, but treat it as a main-body anchor that may later be mirrored or expanded in Appendix A.
- Later continuity editing should consider whether the eleven pattern paragraphs can be tightened without losing the split-market logic.
- Preserve the caveats around secondary exclusion and sublimit signals.
- Do not let R3B import Chapter 2 examples as proof that insurers accept agentic lifecycle transfer.

## Repair Instruction

No R3A repair required. The chapter is accepted with notes because the density is editorially manageable and not structurally blocking.
