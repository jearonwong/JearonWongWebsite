# Coherence and Arc Audit

## Verdict

PASS WITH REVISION NOTES.

## Front Matter Thesis

The Front Matter gives the paper a clear opening thesis: AI risk is not treated as abstractly insurable or uninsurable; it becomes reviewable only when the loss-relevant agentic work object can be bounded, evidenced, reconstructed, and updated. This is the right thesis for WP3.

Revision note: the Front Matter introduces `Agentic Insurability Readiness Model`, while Chapter 25 uses `Agentic Insurability Reasoning Model`. R3J should harmonize the term because `Readiness` can imply a readiness-certification frame that the paper otherwise rejects.

## Part I: Market Reality

Part I successfully starts with market reality instead of internal taxonomy. It shows that the market is already splitting AI risk through cover, exclusions, sublimits, silent exposure, cyber-linked treatment, professional liability ambiguity, D&O/governance concerns, model-performance warranties, and claim reconstruction needs.

Revision note: Chapter 2 is dense but useful. R3J should preserve its source-grounded market signal function while tightening local transitions.

## Part II: Object Layer

Part II is structurally strong. It defines the Insurable Agentic Risk Object as an authored analytical construct and separates the insured legal subject from the loss-relevant work object. Chapters 4-8 build responsibility continuity, underwriting-facing evidence, claim evidence, and hard-to-insure negative space.

Revision note: later chapters repeat some Part II object-field lists. R3J should preserve the first full definition and use shorter references later.

## Part III: WP1/WP2 Translation Layer

Part III does its intended job. WP1 MROs provide lifecycle vocabulary, and WP2 audit evidence chain concepts support reconstruction. Neither is treated as insurance fact, underwriting standard, legal proof, coverage trigger, or insurer-adopted method.

Revision note: after Chapter 13, references to WP1/WP2 can usually be shorter unless a chapter carries fresh boundary risk.

## Part IV: Underwriting-Facing Architecture

Part IV deepens underwriting-facing architecture without becoming underwriting guidance. The exposure inventory, non-pricing exposure variables, renewal/change evidence, and optional reviewer request structure all remain analytical and non-mandatory.

Revision note: Chapters 14, 17, and 18 repeat some evidence architecture lists. R3J should shorten repeated lists while retaining the high-risk boundary notes in Chapters 16 and 18.

## Part V: Post-Loss Responsibility Evidence

Part V moves correctly to post-loss reconstruction, disputes, coverage-boundary questions, remediation, and claims-to-renewal feedback. It does not turn claim reconstruction into claim approval, coverage-boundary analysis into coverage opinion, or remediation closure into settlement/no-liability proof.

Revision note: Chapters 22 and 23 echo Part IV's change/renewal architecture. The overlap is logical, but R3J should sharpen the transition from post-loss remediation to future review.

## Part VI: Final Analytical Models and Conclusion

Part VI provides the expected synthesis. Chapter 24 defines the object model, Chapter 25 defines a non-scoring reasoning model, Chapter 26 gives audience-specific takeaways, Chapter 27 consolidates caveats, and Chapter 28 concludes the body without artifact or release claims.

Revision note: Chapter 28 has solid thesis language. R3J can make the final paragraph more memorable while keeping publication claims out of scope.

## Arc Finding

AI risk noise -> market split -> agentic risk object -> lifecycle evidence -> underwriting-facing reviewability -> post-loss reconstruction -> final analytical model.
