## Verdict

PASS_WITH_TERMINOLOGY_NOTE.

## Agentic Insurability Object Model

Chapter 24 keeps the Agentic Insurability Object Model as an authored analytical model. It connects insured legal subject, loss-relevant agentic work object, authority boundary, responsibility continuity, tool-action consequence, evidence chain, privacy profile, dependency/substitution context, accepted outcome, exception/dispute/remediation state, and renewal/change feedback.

It does not turn the model into model governance, cyber logging, raw traces, audit evidence chain, underwriting checklist, claim file, standard, certification, proof of coverage, proof of insurability, legal liability, or insurer acceptance.

## Agentic Insurability Reasoning Model

Chapter 25 frames the model as non-scoring. The reasoning states are analytical maturity states for reviewability, not insurance outcomes. They do not function as grades, scores, benchmarks, pass/fail tests, rating factors, certification thresholds, readiness certifications, or insurer-adopted methods.

The chapter clearly states that a higher state means better reviewability, not guaranteed insurability.

## Terminology Note

The body should harmonize terminology in R3J. Front Matter uses `Agentic Insurability Readiness Model`, while Chapter 25 uses `Agentic Insurability Reasoning Model`. The safer term is `Reasoning Model` because it avoids implying readiness certification or market acceptance.

## Reviewability Boundary

The draft preserves the core distinctions:

- reviewability is not coverage;
- evidence is not insurance;
- governance is not claim approval;
- auditability is not insurability;
- a non-scoring model is not a score, standard, or certification.

## Result

PASS_WITH_TERMINOLOGY_NOTE. The model framing is strong and safe. R3J should harmonize AIRM language and keep the final models analytical.
