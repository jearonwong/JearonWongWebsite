# WP1 / WP2 Bridge Audit

## Verdict

PASS WITH REPETITION NOTES.

## WP1 Boundary

WP1 MROs remain analytical lifecycle governance vocabulary. The draft does not treat MROs as insurance facts, policy definitions, underwriting standards, coverage triggers, legal proof, certification methods, or insurer-endorsed categories.

Chapter 9 performs the strongest bridge work by mapping MROs to insurability questions while preserving the boundary that MROs help describe the object and do not create coverage.

## WP2 Boundary

WP2 auditability remains reconstruction support, not insurability proof. Chapter 10 clearly separates audit evidence chain concepts from coverage, claim approval, legal liability, and insurer acceptance.

The paper preserves the distinction:

- raw traces are not evidence chains;
- auditability is not insurability;
- reconstruction support is not claim approval;
- evidence reviewability is not coverage.

## MPLP and Validation Lab

MPLP is non-dominant. It is not made the hero of the paper and is not presented as required, standard, certification, insurer-adopted method, or coverage path.

Validation Lab concepts are not turned into certification, legal proof, insurer proof, or market acceptance.

## WP3 Independence

WP3 has its own insurability thesis. It does not read merely as a WP1/WP2 insurance summary. The body uses WP1 and WP2 to support the agentic risk-object argument, then moves into underwriting-facing architecture, post-loss reconstruction, final models, and caveats.

## R3J Recommendations

- Preserve full WP1/WP2 explanation in Front Matter and Part III.
- Shorten repeated WP1/WP2 bridge language in Parts IV-VI when no new boundary risk is introduced.
- Keep WP1 as lifecycle vocabulary only.
- Keep WP2 as auditability/reconstruction vocabulary only.
- Do not add MPLP prominence.

## Result

PASS. The bridge supports WP3 without dominating it. R3J should tune repetition, not repair the underlying concept.
