## How R3A Sets Up Part II

R3A establishes that the insurance market is not making a single yes/no decision about AI. It shows a split-market frame across affirmative AI cover, exclusion/endorsement development, sublimit/cap signals, silent exposure, AI-linked cyber risk, professional liability ambiguity, D&O/governance exposure, claim reconstruction, aggregation/reinsurance concern, model-performance warranty, and the agentic lifecycle gap.

That market reality creates the Part II problem:

The insured legal subject and the agentic risk object are not the same thing.

Part II must now define that separation in concrete insurance and enterprise terms. It should answer:

- Is the relevant legal subject the company, person, officer, professional, vendor, or platform?
- What is the loss-relevant agentic work object?
- How does the work object connect to human role, agent role, corporate responsibility, vendor/platform dependency, and policy line?
- Why is "human in the loop" a review event rather than a responsibility architecture?

- Verisk/ISO supports form-development/boundary signal, not universal exclusion.
- AIG / WR Berkley / Great American exclusion signals remain secondary/caveated until primary filings or policy forms are obtained.
- AI cyber sublimit/cap signals remain secondary/caveated unless primary policy wording is obtained.
- Aon sources support cross-line/silent-exposure context, not coverage determination.
- NIST/CISA support evidence and incident process, not claim approval.
- SEC/NAIC support governance context, not insurance coverage proof for enterprise insureds.
- Geneva/Swiss Re support aggregation/dependency visibility, not actuarial pricing or reinsurer acceptance.
