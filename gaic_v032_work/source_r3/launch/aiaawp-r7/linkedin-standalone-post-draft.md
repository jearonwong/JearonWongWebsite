# LinkedIn Standalone Post Draft

Logs are not audit evidence chains.

That is the core problem I keep coming back to when thinking about AI agents, multi-agent systems, and enterprise governance.

A log may show that a model call happened. It may show a tool was invoked. It may even preserve a final output.

But auditability asks a harder question: can we reconstruct the lifecycle of responsibility?

Who authorized the agentic action? What role boundary applied? What context was used? Which tool action mattered? What exception occurred? What outcome was accepted? How was dispute, remediation, or closure handled?

I published the Agentic AI Auditability & Assurance White Paper 2026 as a public research candidate to explore this gap.

It introduces Agentic AI Auditability, the Agentic Audit Object, Audit Evidence Chain, AARM, and MRO-to-audit-evidence mapping as a way to discuss auditability for AI agent systems beyond model governance alone.

Question for audit, assurance, internal audit, CIO, CTO, and AI governance leaders:

What evidence would you require before trusting an AI agent system's work as reviewable?
