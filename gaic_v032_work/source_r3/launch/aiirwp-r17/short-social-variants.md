## 1. Insurance Angle

AI agents are not the insured subject. The company may be insured; the agent is an agentic risk object that has to be mapped back to roles, responsibility, evidence, and coverage boundaries. The new AIIRWP public research candidate asks what insurers, brokers, and claims teams would need before agentic behavior becomes legible for risk transfer. Link placeholder: `{{AIIRWP_ROUTE_URL}}`

## 2. Enterprise Risk Angle

AI agents are not the insured subject. For enterprise risk leaders, the hard question is whether agent behavior can be reconstructed after a loss event: authority, context, control, tool use, handoff, exception, and closure. AIIRWP frames this as a lifecycle evidence problem, not a hype problem. Link placeholder: `{{AIIRWP_ROUTE_URL}}`

## 3. CTO/CIO Angle

AI agents are not the insured subject. Logs and traces help, but they are not enough if the enterprise cannot explain who authorized the agent, what context it used, and how its actions map to governance and incident evidence. AIIRWP gives CTOs and CIOs a risk-transfer lens for agentic systems. Link placeholder: `{{AIIRWP_ROUTE_URL}}`

## 4. Broker/Underwriter Angle

AI agents are not the insured subject. The underwriting question is not whether a framework emits traces. It is whether the enterprise can connect agent actions to role authority, control evidence, incident reconstruction, and coverage boundaries. AIIRWP is a public research candidate for that evidence conversation. Link placeholder: `{{AIIRWP_ROUTE_URL}}`

## 5. Audit-to-Insurability Angle

AI agents are not the insured subject. Auditability matters, but auditability does not automatically equal insurability. AIIRWP extends the Agentic Lifecycle Governance Industry Series from compliance and assurance into risk transfer, asking what lifecycle evidence would make agent behavior intelligible to insurers and claims teams.
