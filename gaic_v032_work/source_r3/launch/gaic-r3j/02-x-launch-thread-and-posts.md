# X Launch Thread and Posts

## GAIC White Paper Thread

1. AI agent compliance is not only model compliance. It is lifecycle responsibility compliance.

2. Model governance asks whether the model is risky, transparent, evaluated, monitored, and controlled. Agentic governance asks what happened across the lifecycle of work.

3. The missing layer: intent, authority, context, tool action, evidence, responsibility transfer, accepted outcome, rollback, and remediation.

4. I call these Missing Regulatory Objects. If they are not represented, governance becomes a policy layer above an execution system it cannot reconstruct.

5. The Global AI Compliance White Paper 2026 introduces Agentic Lifecycle Governance as a way to name this layer.

6. RCCS-M asks whether regulatory coverage survives through Missing Regulatory Objects. ALCS asks whether the system supports agentic lifecycle conformance.

7. This is not a vendor ranking. It is not procurement guidance. It is not legal compliance proof. It is a source-qualified lifecycle governance framework.

8. MPLP is one protocol path for expressing lifecycle responsibility objects. It is not required, exclusive, certified, regulator-approved, or already an industry standard.

9. The web edition is now readable as HTML, with concept pages, systems mappings, governance mappings, and playbooks connected around it.

10. Start here: https://www.jearonwong.com/research/global-ai-compliance-white-paper-2026/

## Six Standalone Posts

### Post 1

AI agent governance cannot stop at "was the model safe?"

The lifecycle question is:

Who authorized the work, under what boundary, with what evidence, toward what accepted outcome?

https://www.jearonwong.com/concepts/agentic-lifecycle-governance/

### Post 2

Missing Regulatory Objects are the things agentic systems often fail to preserve:

authority, evidence, responsibility transfer, accepted outcome, rollback, remediation.

Without them, compliance cannot reconstruct the work.

https://www.jearonwong.com/concepts/missing-regulatory-objects/

### Post 3

Deterministic Delivery does not mean deterministic LLM output.

It means agentic work is scoped, authorized, evidenced, reviewable, accepted, remediable, and rollbackable.

https://www.jearonwong.com/concepts/deterministic-delivery/

### Post 4

Prompt engineering expresses intent.

Harness Engineering governs execution boundaries.

Those are not the same job.

https://www.jearonwong.com/playbooks/prompt-engineering-vs-harness-engineering/

### Post 5

RCCS-M is not a vendor leaderboard.

It is a way to ask whether regulatory coverage can be expressed through Missing Regulatory Objects in agentic workflows.

https://www.jearonwong.com/concepts/rccs-m/

### Post 6

AI agent incident response needs more than logs.

It needs evidence chain, accepted outcome state, rollback/remediation record, and owner responsibility.

https://www.jearonwong.com/governance/incident-dispute-remediation-closure/

## Quote-Post Style Prompts

1. If someone says "we already have model governance," quote with: "Model governance is necessary. The missing question is whether responsibility survives agentic execution."
2. If someone discusses agent orchestration, quote with: "Orchestration coordinates execution. It does not by itself define accepted outcome, evidence chain, or authority boundary."
3. If someone discusses AI compliance checklists, quote with: "Useful checklist. The next layer is lifecycle responsibility: who authorized, what changed, what evidence survived, who accepted, and how remediation closes."
