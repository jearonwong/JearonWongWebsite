# Medium Article Brief

## Title

AI Agent Compliance Is Not Model Compliance

## Thesis

AI agent and multi-agent system compliance requires lifecycle responsibility objects. Model governance is necessary, but it does not fully answer who authorized agentic work, what context was active, what evidence survived, who accepted the outcome, and how rollback or remediation closes.

## Outline

1. The model-centric governance assumption.
2. Why agentic systems create lifecycle responsibility failures.
3. Missing Regulatory Objects.
4. RCCS-M and ALCS as evaluation language.
5. Deterministic Delivery as lifecycle discipline, not deterministic model output.
6. MPLP as one protocol path.
7. Why this is not legal advice, certification, vendor ranking, or procurement guidance.
8. Where to start in the GAIC web edition and concept map.

## Key Arguments

- Agents perform work across time; compliance needs lifecycle reconstruction, not only output review.
- Logs are not the same as evidence chain.
- Human-in-the-loop is not enough if human responsibility is not mapped to lifecycle objects.
- Accepted outcome is a governance state, not just user satisfaction.
- Substitution across models, runtimes, tools, and vendors requires continuity of authority, evidence, and accepted outcome.
