AI agents are not the insured subject.

The company may be insured. A person, officer, employee, professional service, or business process may sit inside a policy analysis. But the agent itself is usually an agentic risk object, not the legal subject that holds coverage.

That distinction matters because agentic work has to be mapped back to a legal subject, human role, work unit, loss event, and claim evidence chain. A log can show that a system acted. It may not show who had authority, which coverage boundary matters, whether a human actually approved the work, or whether the event can be reconstructed for claims review.

Current AI insurance remains fragmented and conditional: cyber, technology E&O, professional liability, D&O, product liability, and emerging AI endorsements each capture pieces of the problem. None automatically solves agentic responsibility mapping.

My new white paper, Agentic AI Insurability & Risk Transfer White Paper 2026, defines Agentic Insurability Objects and the Agentic Insurability Readiness Model as analytical terms for this gap.
