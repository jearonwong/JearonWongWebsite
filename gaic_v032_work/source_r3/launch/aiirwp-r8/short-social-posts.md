## 2. Logs Are Not Claim Evidence Angle

Logs matter, but logs alone do not make AI agents insurable.

A log may show that an agent called a tool or produced an output. It may not show who had authority, which business process applied, what human role owned the work, or how a loss event maps to a coverage boundary.

## 3. HITL Is Not Responsibility Mapping Angle

"Human in the loop" is not the same as responsibility mapping.

Was the human approving, supervising, reviewing, delegating, monitoring, or only available? Which work unit did they own? What authority did they hold? What evidence would reconstruct that after a loss?

AIIRWP argues that agentic AI risk transfer needs lifecycle evidence, not just HITL labels.

## 4. Insurability Object Angle

Agentic AI insurance discussions need clearer objects.

AIIRWP introduces Agentic Insurability Objects as analytical terms: insured legal subject, agentic risk object, human role, work unit, tool permission, business authority, loss event, evidence chain, dependency layer, and boundary language.

The goal is not to declare systems coverage-ready. It is to make the risk-transfer conversation more precise.
