# WP3-R9 Publication-Grade Rewrite Brief

## Target Title

Agentic AI Insurability & Risk Transfer White Paper 2026

## Core Thesis

AI agents are not the insured legal subject. They are agentic risk objects whose actions must be mapped back to human roles, corporate responsibility, coverage boundaries, and reconstructable lifecycle evidence.

## R10 Rewrite Direction

WP3 must be rewritten as an industry problem definition, explanatory argument, and evidence-backed thesis. It must not be a public rendering of source registers, QA logs, or internal object mapping.

## Writing Principles

- Plain-English first.
- Industry pain first.
- Evidence supports argument; evidence does not replace argument.
- AIO/AIRM appear after the problem is fully established.
- Chapter 0-5 must be substantially expanded.
- Source markers become footnotes, endnotes, or source notes, not raw inline tags.
- Internal governance artifacts are excluded from the public body.
- Tables are selected for reader understanding, not completeness.
- AIO/AIRM are presented as Jearon Wong synthesis / analytical terms, not standards.
- No insurance advice.
- No legal advice.
- No coverage opinion.
- No underwriting standard.
- No claims approval guidance.

## Required Body Ratio

| Content type | Required ratio |
| --- | --- |
| Narrative argument / explanation / industry framing | 65-75% |
| Tables / figures / framework | 15-25% |
| Citations / source notes | 5-10% |
| Internal QA / pipeline / source hygiene tasks in public body | 0% |

## Proposed Argument Architecture

### Part 1 - Industry Problem

- Why AI insurance appears to exist while Agentic AI remains difficult to insure broadly.
- Why cyber, E&O, D&O, and AI-specific coverage sit at the edges of the agentic problem.
- Why companies can see the workflow but insurers need loss-relevant lifecycle evidence.

### Part 2 - Core Answer

- Who is insured: company, person, director, officer, professional, or other legal subject.
- What is the risk object: bounded agentic work unit, not the agent as legal subject.
- Who is responsible: human role, agent role, work unit, evidence, loss event, and legal subject mapping.

### Part 3 - Market Gap

- Existing coverage is fragmented and conditional.
- Exclusions, sublimits, silent AI risk, and policy boundary uncertainty are market signals.
- The missing object is lifecycle claim evidence.

### Part 4 - Framework

- AIO as analytical object layer.
- AIRM as readiness vocabulary.
- Claim Evidence Chain.
- Coverage boundary, exclusion trigger, dependency, aggregation, and dispute readiness.

### Part 5 - Closure

Agentic AI risk transfer begins when agent work becomes bounded, attributable, reconstructable, and disputable. The white paper should define the language for insurability discussion without claiming coverage, underwriting readiness, certification, or insurer acceptance.
