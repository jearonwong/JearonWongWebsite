# WP3-R2 Drafting Packet for R3

**Task ID:** WP3-R2-SOURCE-GROUNDED-OUTLINE
**Mode:** Drafting packet only
**Boundary:** No body draft yet. This file tells R3 how to write safely.

## Target Shape

- Recommended first internal draft length: 60 to 90 pages.
- Draft order: Chapters 0-3 first, then 4-5, then 6-7, then 8-11, then 12-15, then 16, then appendices.
- Keep the first draft internal and source-grounded; do not optimize for publication polish yet.

## Tone

- Cold, precise, evidence-oriented, and direct.
- Plain-English first, technical language second.
- No legal advice tone, no insurance advice tone, no consulting filler, no vendor marketing tone.
- Use category-defining sentences, not sales language.

## Citation Rules

- Every major factual claim needs a source ID or an explicit synthesis label.
- Technical docs are context sources, not proof of insurance meaning.
- Internal WP1/WP2 files may anchor translation claims, but the text must say they are internal source truth.
- Source-adjacent inference must stay attached to its source IDs and must not be turned into universal language.
- If a source supports only a narrow point, keep the sentence narrow.

## Synthesis Marking Rules

- First use of any AIO or AIRM concept should say "WP3 author synthesis" or equivalent.
- If a paragraph mixes source-backed and synthesized points, mark the synthesized sentence explicitly.
- Do not present AIO or AIRM as external standards, insurer standards, or accepted market terminology.

## Mandatory Chapter Pattern

1. Plain-English problem.
2. Why the current AI/insurance discussion fails.
3. Lifecycle object needed.
4. Insurance use case.
5. WP1/WP2 mapping.
6. Boundary statement.
7. Table or figure.

## Mandatory Opening Logic for Chapters 0-3

- Chapter 0: Start with what insurance must know in ordinary language before any AI jargon appears.
- Chapter 1: Start with the insured subject question, not with the model.
- Chapter 2: Start with what exactly is being covered, not with product naming.
- Chapter 3: Start with how responsibility is mapped, not with who is blamed.

## Table Placement Guidance

- Place the table after the concept is introduced, not before it.
- Keep comparison tables as comparison tables.
- Use landscape only when it materially improves readability.
- Use split matrices before row-card fallback for dense comparative tables.
- Do not let a comparison table become a decorative card set.

## Forbidden Phrases

- insurance advice
- underwriting standard
- coverage opinion
- claims approval framework
- legal liability determination
- certification
- insurer acceptance
- required by MPLP
- Validation Lab certification
- vendor ranking
- procurement recommendation
- broad market consensus
- AI agents are legal insured subjects
- WP3 is published
- WP3 is final

## Required Safe Phrases

- necessary layer, not sufficient insurability layer
- technical trace, not claim evidence by itself
- tool permission, not coverage authority
- workflow completion, not insured outcome
- vendor assurance, not causality reconstruction
- source-backed implication
- WP3 author synthesis
- reviewable evidence chain
- dispute-ready claim package
- reconstructable lifecycle evidence

## R3 Review Checklist

- Every chapter opens in plain English.
- Every chapter states what the framework does not claim.
- Every chapter maps back to WP1 or WP2.
- No chapter overclaims insurer acceptance, coverage, or compliance.
- No chapter converts internal analytical models into external standards.
- No chapter uses a source only as a marketing proxy.
