## Content Summary

### Section 10.4: Cybersecurity Controls Integration

**10.4.1: Why Cybersecurity and Lifecycle Governance Intersect**
- Agent execution creates attack surfaces: prompt injection, tool misuse, credential theft, data exfiltration
- Lifecycle governance provides authorization and evidence layer for cybersecurity controls
- Reference frameworks: OWASP LLM Top 10, NIST AI RMF, MITRE ATLAS
- Cybersecurity and lifecycle governance are complementary, not substitutes

**10.4.2: Cybersecurity Control Mapping Table — T-10-03**
- 8-row table mapping cybersecurity control domains to agentic attack surfaces, lifecycle governance objects, MRO dimensions, and evidence outputs
- Covers: Identity/Access, Prompt Injection, Tool Misuse, Credential Management, Data Exfiltration, Audit Trail Integrity, Supply-Chain Integrity, Incident Response Linkage

**10.4.3: Integration Note**
- Boundary statement: governance crosswalk, not cybersecurity audit or penetration testing guide
- Neither cybersecurity nor lifecycle governance replaces the other

### Section 10.5: Board Reporting and Governance Escalation

**10.5.1: What Boards Need to Know About Agentic AI**
- Boards increasingly responsible for AI risk oversight (EU AI Act Art. 9, NIST AI RMF Govern, SEC cyber disclosure precedent)
- Agentic AI creates board-level concerns: lifecycle accountability gaps, unresolved disputes, remediation failures, privacy incidents
- Key question: Can board receive lifecycle status as structured, auditable, reproducible report?

**10.5.2: Board-Level Reporting Elements — T-10-04**
- 6-row table defining board reporting elements mapped to lifecycle objects, frequency, escalation triggers, regulatory drivers
- Covers: Lifecycle Accountability Summary, Open Dispute Register, Remediation Closure Rate, Privacy Incident Summary, Vendor/Tool Substitution Log, Drift and Continuous Improvement Status

**10.5.3: Escalation Note**
- What triggers board escalation vs. management reporting
- Lifecycle objects provide structured data for escalation decisions
- Boundary statement: governance crosswalk, not corporate governance framework or securities law opinion

### Section 10.6: Procurement Scorecard Application

**10.6.1: Procurement Lifecycle Governance Requirements**
- When acquiring agentic AI tools/platforms/services, procurement teams need lifecycle governance criteria
- Reference: EU AI Act supply-chain obligations (Art. 25, 28), DORA ICT third-party risk, NIST AI RMF supply-chain risk
- Key criteria: evidence export, tool-action liability boundaries, agent identity lifecycle documentation

**10.6.2: Procurement Scorecard Table — T-10-05**
- 8-row table defining procurement criteria mapped to lifecycle objects, acceptable evidence, risk if absent, regulatory references
- Covers: Agent Identity Lifecycle, Tool-Action Liability Boundary, Evidence Export Capability, Privacy Data-Flow Map, Incident Response Support, Vendor Substitution Support, Drift Monitoring Capability, Dispute Resolution Support

**10.6.3: Scorecard Note**
- Governance reference tool, not vendor certification or audit requirement
- Procurement teams should adapt to specific risk profile and regulatory environment
- Boundary statement: not legal procurement specification or vendor audit

### Section 10.7: Ongoing Monitoring and Continuous Improvement

**10.7.1: Why Monitoring Must Be Lifecycle-Integrated**
- Traditional observability monitors execution metrics; lifecycle governance monitors accountability continuity
- MRO-09 (Lifecycle Drift Detection), MRO-10 (Continuous Improvement Feedback Loop) are relevant dimensions
- Lifecycle monitoring asks: Are responsibility assignments valid? Are evidence chains complete? Are tool-action liability boundaries operative?

**10.7.2: Monitoring Indicators Table — T-10-06**
- 8-row table defining lifecycle monitoring indicators mapped to lifecycle objects, alert conditions, MRO dimensions, remediation paths
- Covers: Responsibility Drift, Evidence Chain Break, Tool-Action Boundary Violation, Human-Role Absence, Dispute Backlog, Privacy Data-Flow Deviation, Vendor Substitution Alert, Continuous Improvement Stall

**10.7.3: Continuous Improvement Note**
- Lifecycle monitoring feeds back into ALCS lifecycle records, MRO scores, RCCS coverage assessments
- Governance loop: authorization → execution → evidence → review → remediation → improvement → re-authorization
- Boundary statement: governance crosswalk, not real-time monitoring product or SRE framework

### Section 10.8: Chapter Closure and Bridge to Chapter 16

**10.8.1: What Chapter 10 Has Established**
- Summary of enterprise control crosswalk achievements
- How it connects lifecycle governance objects to audit, insurance, procurement, board, cybersecurity language
- Why enterprise adoption requires this crosswalk layer

**10.8.2: What Chapter 16 Will Add**
- Chapter 16: Boundary to Companion Papers: Evidence, Assurance, and Insurability
- Bridge: Chapter 10 provides control language; Chapter 16 provides boundary to companion papers
- Chapter 16 will cover: evidence-based validation patterns, assurance frameworks, insurability considerations, companion paper scope
