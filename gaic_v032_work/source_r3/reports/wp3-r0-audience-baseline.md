# WP3-R0 Audience Baseline

**Task ID:** WP3-R0-INSURABILITY-ARCHITECTURE-BASELINE  
**Mode:** Planning-only  
**Status:** Audience architecture only. No final prose, no market claims.

## Reader Assumption

WP3 must write for intelligent readers who may not know agentic AI lifecycle theory, MAS, MROs, MPLP, or insurance evidence objects. Each concept must start in plain English, then introduce technical language.

## Primary Audience

| Audience | Question they bring | What WP3 should answer | What WP3 must not overclaim | WP1/WP2/WP4 relation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insurers | What exactly are we being asked to insure when an AI agent acts? | How to distinguish insured legal subject from insurable agentic risk object and what evidence is needed for review. | No underwriting standard, pricing model, coverage opinion, or claim approval. | WP1 supplies lifecycle objects; WP2 supplies evidence-chain discipline; WP4 later translates controls. |
| Reinsurers | Where could correlated agentic losses accumulate? | How agentic workflows, vendors, models, tools, and delegated authorities may create accumulation views needing source research. | No reinsurance model or systemic loss estimate. | WP1 failure scenarios and MROs map into WP3 aggregation questions; WP2 audit evidence helps reconstruct correlated events. |
| Brokers | How do we explain agentic AI risk to markets without overpromising coverage? | A vocabulary for legal subject, risk object, coverage boundary, claim evidence chain, and dispute-ready packages. | No placement advice, policy wording, or guarantee of market appetite. | WP1/WP2 give source-grounded governance and evidence terms; WP4 later helps operationalize. |
| Underwriting teams | What evidence would make an agentic exposure reviewable? | Evidence categories for bounded work units, authority, tools, oversight, loss scenario, controls, and dependencies. | No mandatory underwriting checklist or acceptance threshold. | WP1 MRO/ALCS and WP2 evidence objects become underwriting evidence inputs. |
| Claims teams | How can a loss involving agents be reconstructed? | Claim evidence chain and loss event reconstruction objects. | No claim approval framework or legal causation finding. | WP2 Audit Evidence Chain maps to WP3 Claim Evidence Chain; WP1 MRO-16 supports closure. |
| Risk engineering teams | What controls and evidence should be examined before a loss? | Lifecycle evidence architecture for authority, delegation, tool use, outcome acceptance, exceptions, and remediation. | No certification or audit opinion. | WP1 provides control object layer; WP2 provides reviewability; WP4 later provides implementation plane. |
| Cyber insurance teams | How does agentic AI change cyber-style evidence and aggregation questions? | Source-research requirements for cyber exclusions, sublimits, LLM abuse, dependencies, and evidence expectations. | No current market fact without R1 source grounding. | WP1/2 help define evidence boundaries; WP4 later maps security controls. |
| Technology E&O / professional liability teams | When agentic work affects professional service delivery, what needs to be evidenced? | Responsibility map, work-unit boundary, accepted outcome, and dispute package concepts. | No professional liability opinion or policy interpretation. | WP1 human-role mapping and WP2 assurance boundary inform WP3 review objects. |
| Enterprise CRO | How do we make agentic AI risk transferable rather than merely deployed? | AIRM readiness vocabulary and AIO object layer for risk-transfer readiness planning. | No guarantee of insurability or coverage. | WP1 is governance root; WP2 is evidence root; WP4 later becomes implementation synthesis. |
| Enterprise CFO | What is the risk-transfer gap that affects balance-sheet planning? | Plain-English explanation of why logs and vendor assurances are insufficient for claim-ready risk transfer. | No financial advice, pricing model, or capital recommendation. | WP3 translates WP1/WP2 into finance-facing risk-transfer questions. |
| General counsel | How do responsibility, policy boundaries, and evidence fit without assigning liability? | Distinction between responsibility mapping and legal liability determination. | No legal advice, liability opinion, or coverage opinion. | WP1 MROs preserve legal boundary; WP2 evidentiary discipline avoids overclaim. |
| Board risk committees | What should directors understand before approving scaled agentic deployment? | The governance-to-evidence-to-risk-transfer logic and why broad insurability needs lifecycle evidence. | No D&O advice, coverage guarantee, or certification claim. | WP3 helps board readers connect WP1 compliance and WP2 auditability to risk transfer. |

## Style Implication

Every chapter must open with the reader's practical pain: "If an agent acts, who is covered, what is covered, and what evidence proves the path from authority to loss?" Technical terms come after that plain-English problem.
