# GAIC-R4C Guide 2 Argument Architecture

**Status:** Planning baseline
**Date:** May 17, 2026
**Boundary:** Planning-only. This file defines argument structure; it does not draft Guide 2 body text or provide legal advice.

## Core Thesis

Agentic lifecycle governance is the operating discipline that turns regulatory language and enterprise AI policy into responsibility mappings, evidence obligations, authorization boundaries, accepted outcome records, and remediation workflows.

## Core Problem

Traditional AI policy / governance committee structures are insufficient for AI Agent / MAS because agentic systems involve delegated authority, tool actions, human/agent role boundaries, dynamic plans, cross-project reuse, vendor/runtime substitution, evidence retention, privacy constraints, and remediation closure.

## Argument Stack

| Layer | Argument | WP1 / WP2 dependency | Boundary |

| Policy-only AI governance fails for agentic systems | Policies without lifecycle objects cannot reconstruct responsibility in delegated agent work. | WP1 Chapter 2; R4A thesis. | Do not say policy is useless. |

## Objection Handling

| Objection | Answer direction | Boundary to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Is this legal advice? | No. Guide 2 should provide an operating model for policy-to-evidence governance; legal interpretation remains with qualified counsel. | Do not provide jurisdiction-specific legal conclusions. |
| Does this prove compliance? | No. It structures governance evidence and responsibility workflows; compliance proof depends on law, controls, implementation, and professional review. | Do not claim legal compliance proof. |
| Can existing AI governance committees handle this? | They can own it, but they need lifecycle objects, evidence requests, authority governance, and remediation closure processes. | Do not dismiss existing committees. |
| Is this just internal controls? | It builds on controls but adds agentic lifecycle objects for delegated work, tool actions, accepted outcomes, privacy-bound evidence, and closure. | Do not replace internal control frameworks. |
| Do we need MPLP? | No. MPLP is one optional protocol path; Guide 2 remains protocol-neutral. | Do not claim MPLP is required or industry standard. |
| How does this relate to Guide 1? | Guide 1 explains how to build audit-ready systems; Guide 2 explains how to govern them through roles, policy, evidence, and review workflows. | Do not turn Guide 2 into technical implementation manual. |
| How does this relate to audit? | Guide 2 prepares evidence and governance workflows for internal audit, external audit, assurance planning, board review, and qualified reviewers. | Do not claim audit opinion or audit standard status. |
| How does this prepare for insurance / WP3? | It creates the responsibility, authority, evidence, remediation, and substitution records that WP3 can later analyze as insurable risk objects. | Do not claim insurance guarantee or underwriting outcome. |
