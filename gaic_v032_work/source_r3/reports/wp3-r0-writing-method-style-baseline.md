# WP3-R0 Writing Method and Style Baseline

**Task ID:** WP3-R0-INSURABILITY-ARCHITECTURE-BASELINE  
**Mode:** Planning-only  
**Status:** Method baseline only. No whitepaper body text is drafted.

## Required Method

WP3 must follow the GAIC/AIAAWP writing method:

- Source-grounded.
- Chapter-level source register.
- Synthesis marked clearly.
- Boundary-sensitive.
- No overclaim.
- No unsupported market claim.
- Strong but precise language.
- Plain English first, technical language second.
- Each chapter begins with reader pain or a concrete question.
- Each concept is introduced as if the reader does not already know the topic.
- Each chapter maps back to the root whitepaper.
- Each chapter clarifies what the framework does not claim.
- No generic consulting filler.
- No product marketing.
- No vendor ranking.

## Tone

WP3 tone must be:

- Cold.
- Precise.
- Evidence-oriented.
- Direct.
- Category-defining.
- Not academic fluff.
- Not legal advice.
- Not insurance advice.

## Mandatory Chapter Pattern

Every future chapter must follow this pattern:

1. Plain-English problem.
2. Why current AI/insurance discussion fails.
3. Lifecycle object needed.
4. Insurance use case.
5. WP1/WP2 mapping.
6. Boundary statement.
7. Table or figure.

## Source Discipline

Future drafting must distinguish:

- Source-grounded external fact.
- WP1 internal source truth.
- WP2 internal source truth.
- WP3 author synthesis.
- Hypothetical example.
- Boundary statement.
- Future implementation bridge.

No market fact can enter a chapter unless the source register identifies where it came from and what it is allowed to support.

## Plain-English First Rule

Each concept must begin with a plain-English sentence before technical language. Examples:

- "The company may be insured. The AI agent usually is not."
- "A log says something happened. Claim evidence helps reconstruct why it happened, who was responsible for the workflow, and how it connects to a loss."
- "A model name is not an exposure boundary."
- "An approval click is not a full responsibility map."

## Boundary Language Requirements

Each chapter must include a boundary block that states what the chapter does not claim. Depending on chapter scope, this must include:

- Not insurance advice.
- Not legal advice.
- Not underwriting standard.
- Not actuarial pricing model.
- Not coverage opinion.
- Not claims approval.
- Not legal liability determination.
- Not certification.
- Not insurer acceptance.
- Not MPLP-required.
- Not Validation Lab certification.
- Not vendor ranking.
- Not procurement recommendation.

## Publication Style Requirements

If WP3 later reaches publication-candidate generation, it must inherit:

- GAIC/AIAAWP restrained research-whitepaper visual family.
- HTML web-reading profile.
- PDF A4 publication profile.
- DOCX internal editable derivative profile.
- Metadata, JSON-LD, citation, manifest, checksum, and boundary QA discipline.
