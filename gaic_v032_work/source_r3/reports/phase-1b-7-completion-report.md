## Content Summary

### Chapter 17: Adoption Roadmap

**Section 17.0: Why Adoption Roadmap Matters**
- Positions adoption roadmap as practical necessity, not theoretical framework
- Roadmap is not a procurement guide, certification checklist, or legal compliance procedure
- Goal: provide staged adoption path for enterprises to address lifecycle governance gap

**Section 17.1: Maturity Assessment and Gap Analysis**
- Created T-17-01: Adoption Maturity Levels
  - 5 levels: Level 0 (Unaware) → Level 1 (Aware) → Level 2 (Pilot) → Level 3 (Integrated) → Level 4 (Scaled)
  - Each level mapped to enterprise state, lifecycle governance capability, typical gap, and recommended next step
- Interpretation: Most enterprises deploying agentic AI systems are at Level 0 or Level 1

**Section 17.2: Pilot Project Selection**
- Created T-17-02: Pilot Project Selection Criteria
  - 7 criteria: Bounded scope, Measurable outcomes, Regulatory relevance, Executive sponsorship, Failure scenario relevance, Integration opportunity, Rollback capability
  - Each criterion mapped to why it matters, good pilot signal, and avoid if
- Interpretation: Good pilots are bounded, measurable, regulated, sponsored, relevant to failure scenarios, integrated with existing controls, and rollback-capable

**Section 17.3: Integration with Existing Enterprise Controls**
- Created T-17-03: Existing Enterprise Control Integration
  - 8 existing controls: Internal Audit, Privacy/Data Governance, Security/Access Management, Procurement/Vendor Management, Incident Response, Compliance/Regulatory Reporting, Model Risk Management, Business Continuity/Rollback
  - Each control mapped to lifecycle governance extension, relevant object, and evidence artifact
- Interpretation: Lifecycle governance extends existing enterprise controls rather than replacing them

**Section 17.4: Operationalizing Lifecycle Responsibility Objects**
- Created T-17-04: Adoption Roadmap by Stage
  - 7 stages: Stage 0 (Inventory) → Stage 1 (Authority) → Stage 2 (Responsibility) → Stage 3 (Evidence) → Stage 4 (Privacy) → Stage 5 (Validation) → Stage 6 (Remediation) → Stage 7 (Scale)
  - Each stage mapped to goal, key actions, output, and success indicator
- Interpretation: Seven-stage roadmap provides practical path from lifecycle governance awareness to scaled operational capability

**Section 17.5: Measuring Progress and Demonstrating Conformance**
- Created T-17-05: RCCS/ALCS Use in Adoption
  - 6 use cases: Gap analysis, Pilot project baseline, System evaluation, Audit readiness, Evidence-based validation, Continuous improvement
  - Each use case mapped to RCCS role, ALCS role, and boundary
- Interpretation: RCCS and ALCS are analytical tools that help enterprises measure progress and identify gaps; they are not legal compliance scores, certifications, or regulatory approvals

**Section 17.6: Scaling from Pilot to Production**
- 7 scaling recommendations: Extend pilot learnings, Integrate with existing enterprise controls, Operationalize all lifecycle responsibility objects, Implement continuous improvement, Prepare for evidence-based validation, Use Chapter 15 scenarios as test cases, Contribute to industry standards
- Scaling is continuous process, not one-time event

**Section 17.7: Bridge to Conclusion**

- Sets up Chapter 18 conclusion

## Boundary Discipline Applied

### Framework Positioning
- Not a legal compliance procedure, certification program, or regulatory approval process
- Governance framework that helps enterprises identify lifecycle governance gaps and operationalize lifecycle responsibility objects
- Enterprises must interpret framework within their legal, regulatory, and operational context

### RCCS/ALCS Positioning
- Analytical tools that help enterprises measure progress and identify gaps
- Not legal compliance scores, certifications, or regulatory approvals
- High RCCS/ALCS does not prove legal compliance; low RCCS/ALCS does not prove non-compliance

### MPLP Positioning
- One lifecycle protocol path that maps strongly to ALCS dimensions
- Not required for compliance, not the only correct path, not regulator-approved, not certification program

### Validation Lab Positioning
- One non-certifying evidence adjudication example that demonstrates evidence-based validation pattern
- Not certification authority, not the only validation path, not regulator-approved

### System Mappings Positioning
- Analytical tools for system comparison by RCCS/ALCS posture and lifecycle gaps
- Not product rankings, not certifications, not procurement recommendations, not vendor endorsements

### Risk Management Positioning
- Lifecycle governance addresses lifecycle governance gaps that create predictable enterprise failures
- Lifecycle governance supports risk management but does not eliminate all risk
- Not claims that agentic AI is uniquely unsafe

### Completeness Positioning
- Paper defines sixteen Missing Regulatory Objects and provides framework for lifecycle governance
- Not complete; additional lifecycle responsibility objects may emerge as agentic AI capabilities expand
- Does not replace legal review or regulatory interpretation
