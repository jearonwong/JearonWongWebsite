# WP3-R1 Current AI Insurance Market Map

**Task ID:** WP3-R1-INSURABILITY-SOURCE-RESEARCH
**Mode:** Source research only
**Boundary:** This report maps source-supported market signals. It is not insurance advice, a coverage opinion, underwriting guidance, actuarial pricing guidance, claim approval guidance, vendor ranking, or procurement recommendation.

## Narrative Summary

The source record supports a cautious market map:

- AI is already visible inside insurance operations and regulation, especially through insurer governance expectations for AI systems. NAIC's model bulletin is the strongest regulatory source for insurer use of AI and documentation expectations. Sources: INS-01, INS-02, INS-03.
- AI-specific cover exists at the market edge. Munich Re's aiSure and related GenAI materials describe AI performance/error insurance; QBE announced AI-focused cyber coverages for AI regulatory exposure and LLMjacking; broker and industry association sources describe demand and experimentation. Sources: MKT-01, MKT-02, MKT-03, MKT-04, MKT-06, MKT-08.
- Existing lines may respond to some AI-linked losses depending on facts and policy terms: cyber for data breach/ransomware/AI-linked misuse, E&O for professional-service mistakes, D&O for governance/securities exposure, and CGL or media/liability lines for certain injury, property, or content claims. Sources: INS-07, INS-08, INS-09, INS-10, MKT-05, MKT-06, MKT-07.
- Exclusion, sublimit, silent-risk, and boundary questions are visible but not settled as a universal market practice. Public sources support cautious statements about fragmented policy response and insurability challenges, not broad claims that "the industry excludes AI." Sources: INS-05, MKT-05, MKT-08, CYB-02.
- Broad agentic AI lifecycle risk transfer remains under-supported by current market evidence because the sources do not show a standardized object layer that binds legal subject, human role, agent/MAS role, authority, work unit, loss event, causality, dependencies, remediation, and a dispute-ready claim evidence chain. This final sentence is WP3 author synthesis grounded in INS-01 through INS-10, MKT-01 through MKT-08, CLAIM-01 through CLAIM-03, TECH-01 through TECH-05, and INT-01 through INT-07.

## What Appears Insurable Today

- Narrow AI performance or AI error exposure where a product like aiSure or bespoke AI cover is arranged. Sources: MKT-01, MKT-02.
- AI-linked cyber events, including LLMjacking, where a cyber policy or endorsement expressly addresses the exposure. Sources: MKT-03, MKT-04, INS-09, INS-10.
- AI-assisted professional-service mistakes only if the facts fit the applicable professional liability / E&O policy. Sources: INS-07, MKT-06.
- Corporate governance and disclosure exposure around AI only as a D&O/corporate risk-transfer question, not as direct agent insurance. Sources: INS-08, MKT-07, CLAIM-03.

## What Is Narrowly Insurable or Conditional

- Model performance, drift, hallucination, and AI output losses are visible in AI-specific product materials but remain product-specific and fact-specific. Sources: MKT-01, MKT-02, MKT-08.
- Regulatory fines, defense costs, or AI-related regulatory investigations may be contemplated in specific coverages, but cannot be generalized. Sources: MKT-03, MKT-06.
- Cyber business interruption or service-fee losses may be covered where a cyber policy defines the peril and loss category. Sources: INS-09, INS-10, MKT-03, MKT-04.

## What Is Excluded, Sublimited, or Unclear

- Exact AI exclusions and sublimits are not sufficiently visible in public R1 sources to support a broad market claim.
- Sources support a cautious statement that limits, sublimits, exclusions, and silent-risk carve-outs are active boundary questions. Sources: INS-05, MKT-05, MKT-08, CYB-02.
- R2 should write "unclear, disputed, or policy-specific" unless later sources provide precise forms or carrier filings.

## Why This Does Not Yet Equal Broad Agentic AI Lifecycle Risk Transfer

Current sources show market experimentation, AI governance expectations, cyber threat adaptation, and multi-line exposure mapping. They do not show a broad, standardized risk-transfer structure for autonomous or semi-autonomous agentic work units. WP3 should therefore ground the thesis this way:

> Existing insurance can touch AI-related losses, but broad agentic AI insurability needs a lifecycle object layer that makes the risk unit, authority path, responsibility map, dependency chain, loss event, causality, remediation, and claim evidence reconstructable.

This is source-grounded author synthesis, not an insurer or regulator standard.
